# Colour Palette

**Date:** 2026-08-05

## The system

Neutral, plus one accent. Nothing else.

**Neutrals come from the system, not from us.** `.primary`, `.secondary`,
`.tertiary`, `Color(.systemBackground)`, `Color(.systemGroupedBackground)` and
their kin already flip correctly between light and dark, already respond to
Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency, and already match what a user sees
everywhere else on their phone. Hard-coding a grey scale would mean owning all
of that by hand and getting some of it wrong.

**The accent is ours.** It is defined once, in
`Evenstar/Evenstar/Assets.xcassets/AccentColor.colorset`, which the build
setting `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_GLOBAL_ACCENT_COLOR_NAME = AccentColor` makes the
app-wide tint. Everything that reads `Color.accentColor`, `.tint`, or takes a
prominent button style picks it up with no further wiring.

## The accent

**Orchid** — pink with a clear violet in it.

| Appearance | sRGB | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Light | 0.776, 0.294, 0.820 | `#C64BD1` |
| Dark | 0.894, 0.549, 0.941 | `#E48CF0` |

Two values, not one. A single colour cannot serve both: on white it has to be
dark enough to hold a white label on top of it, and on black that same colour
reads as a bruise. The dark variant is the lighter, more luminous of the pair —
the usual direction, since contrast on a dark background comes from luminance.

Two alternates were rendered beside it on both appearances and rejected only for
being further from the brief, not for being wrong. Either is a one-file swap:

| | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| Magenta (more pink) | `#D63FA6` | `#FF74C8` |
| Violet (more purple) | `#A855E8` | `#C99AFF` |

## Where the accent appears

Deliberately narrow. It marks *state the user set* and *the one action a screen
is asking for* — not decoration.

- The armed repeat mode in the expanded player. Off is `.secondary`, armed is
  accent; the glyph alone cannot carry it, since off and all share a symbol.
- The empty library's icon and its Import Music button.
- Any future `.borderedProminent` button.

It is **not** used for the selected tab, which is `Color.primary` at full
opacity against 0.6 for the rest, or for the scrubber and volume bars, which are
`.label`. Those are all still black-and-white on purpose: the bottom bar and the
transport controls are the app's furniture, and tinting furniture is how an
accent stops meaning anything.

## What is not in this palette

No semantic red/green/amber set, no elevation scale, no named greys. When the
first real need arrives — a destructive confirmation, an error state — take the
system's (`.red`) rather than inventing one, and add it here only if it needs to
differ.
